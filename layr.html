Physical systems shaped by digital process

LAYR is a design‑led hardware studio exploring the intersection of industrial form, small‑batch manufacturing, and digital representation.

The studio operates at the boundary between speculative product design and physically fabricated artefacts. Objects are developed with consumer‑grade manufacturing constraints in mind from inception, allowing material behaviour, tolerances, and assembly logic to inform form, proportion, and detail.

Rather than pursuing mass‑manufactured scalability, LAYR focuses on limited‑run, fabrication‑aware products where process is treated as an active design layer rather than a downstream concern.

Design Principles

  • Fabrication‑aware by default
    All objects are designed with explicit awareness of consumer‑grade manufacturing processes, particularly FDM 3D printing.
  • Constraint as material
    Limitations in tooling, tolerance, and assembly are treated as productive inputs rather than obstacles to be hidden or abstracted away.
  • Small‑batch over mass appeal
    Products are conceived for limited production runs, prioritising clarity of intent and physical presence over scalability.
  • Digital representation as artefact
    Animation, web presentation, and documentation are considered integral outputs of the design process, not purely promotional layers.

Studio Focus

Fabrication & 3D Printing

Objects are developed specifically for consumer‑grade FDM printing, with PETG used as a baseline material. Wall thicknesses, tolerances, and fastening strategies are informed by iterative physical testing rather than idealised CAD assumptions.

This approach prioritises repeatability and reliability over surface perfection, embracing the material qualities and limitations inherent to small‑batch digital fabrication.

Industrial & Cultural References

LAYR draws inspiration from retromodern industrial design, early consumer electronics, and utilitarian hardware objects where function is visually legible.

Rather than pursuing novelty for its own sake, form language is developed through proportion, restraint, and an emphasis on long‑term durability.

Digital Representation

Digital outputs are treated as artefacts in their own right. Animation, web presentation, and documentation form a parallel layer of the design process rather than a promotional afterthought.

Scroll‑based interaction is used to encourage slow inspection, mirroring how a physical object might be handled and examined.
